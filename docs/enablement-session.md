# Running the enablement session

A companion to the Figma Slides deck **“A product team you can call on”** — sixteen
slides for introducing this repository to a product design team, and then to the wider
product team around it.

This document is the presenter’s half. The deck carries the argument; this carries the
reasoning behind the order it is made in, what to do in the room, and what people
usually push back on. Every slide also has speaker notes in the Figma file, which are
the short form of what follows.

It is written for an agency: several client accounts, a small permanent team, and
people who join mid-engagement and need to be useful quickly.

## What the session is trying to do

Four jobs, and they are not equally hard:

| Job | Difficulty | Where it happens |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness — this exists | Easy | Slides 1–3 |
| Knowledge sharing — here is what it actually does | Moderate | Slides 6–7, 10–12 |
| Enablement — you can use it on Monday | Hard | Slide 7 live, slide 15, the drop-in |
| Induction — a new joiner can be brought up this way | Hardest | The recording, and this document |

Awareness is nearly free and worth almost nothing on its own. Enablement is the
expensive one, and it happens in the demo and the follow-up, not in the deck.

## Run of show

Thirty minutes, with the ask at twenty.

| Minutes | Slides | What is happening |
|---|---|---|
| 0–4 | 1–4 | The problem, then the honest boundaries. Buy permission for the rest |
| 4–6 | 5 | Pivot. Narrow the ask to five skills |
| 6–14 | 6–8 | The design bench, a **live** demo, and the critique separation |
| 14–15 | 9 | Pivot. Widen past design |
| 15–19 | 10–12 | The wider bench, the shared cycle, the Project Point of Truth |
| 19–22 | 13–14 | The client argument, then the guardrails |
| 22–25 | 15–16 | How to install, and the ask |
| 25–30 | — | Argument. This is the part worth protecting |

If you are running short, cut slides 10 and 13 — not 4, not 7, not 14.

## Why the deck is ordered this way

**Design first, then everything else.** You are the lead product designer. Your
authority is in the room only for the first half, and it is spent buying attention for
the second. Opening with all twenty-eight skills spends credibility you have not
earned yet on disciplines you do not own. Slide 9 is the hinge, and it is deliberately
almost empty so the change of scope registers.

**The problem before the thing.** Slides 2 and 3 are in that order on purpose. A tool
introduced before a felt problem gets evaluated as a preference. A tool introduced
after one gets evaluated as an answer.

**Say what it is not, early and unprompted.** Slide 4 is the highest-leverage slide in
the deck. A design team’s first question is whether this is aimed at them, and until
that is answered nobody is listening to anything else. Volunteering the limits costs
nothing and buys the remaining twelve slides. The same move repeats at slide 14 for
the client-facing risks.

**Demonstrate rather than describe.** Slide 7 exists to be replaced by a live run
against a real problem from a live account. Then disagree with the output in front of
everyone. That single act does more for adoption than the rest of the deck, because it
shows the intended posture: this is a colleague to argue with, not an oracle to obey.

**Ask for something small.** Slide 16 asks for one real task this week, not a pilot
programme. Programmes need approval; tasks need ten minutes.

## What people push back on

**“Is this going to replace us?”** No, and slide 4 says so before anyone has to ask.
The honest answer is that it is poor at taste, client relationships and
accountability, which is most of what the job actually is. Concede the limits quickly
and specifically — a defended overclaim costs more than the claim was worth.

**“How is this different from just asking Claude?”** The output contract. A role
returns states, labelled assumptions, accessibility implications and a validation step
whether or not you remembered to ask for them. Consistency is the product, not
capability.

**“Twenty-eight is too many to learn.”** You are asking for five. The routing brain
covers the rest when you cannot name the discipline you want.

**“Is the Project Point of Truth not just a wiki page?”** It sits beside the code,
every skill reads it before working, and its entries carry states — a confirmed fact
and an unproven assumption are not stored as the same kind of thing. Nothing is
confirmed without a person approving the exact wording.

**“What do we tell clients?”** Slide 13 is that answer, and slide 14 is the governance
behind it. Never be coy: yes, here is how it is governed, here is who signs it off.
The project-agnostic point matters most to whoever reviews the contract — no client’s
context travels to another account.

**“Who maintains these?”** We do. They are in our repository and anyone in the room
can change one with a pull request. That answer does more for ownership than any
feature on any slide.

## Adapting it

**Design team only (the first run).** As written. Sixteen slides, demo at 7.

**Whole product team (the second run).** Lead with slide 9’s framing, compress
slides 6–8 into one, and give slides 10–12 the time you took back. Engineering and
delivery will care most about the pipeline column on slide 10 and about gates on
slide 11.

**New joiner induction.** Slides 3, 6, 11, 12 and 15, in that order, with slide 15 as
a sit-down rather than a slide. Skip the persuasion — a new joiner has nothing to be
persuaded out of.

**Client-facing.** Slides 11, 13 and 14 only. Never present slides 2 or 16 to a
client; one is our staffing problem and the other is our internal ask.

## After the session

Enablement fails in the gap between interest and setup, so close it the same week:

1. Run a thirty-minute drop-in for installation. Do not rely on slide 15 alone.
2. Pick one live account and run one gate on it, with you in the room.
3. Collect what it got wrong. That list is what turns a general-purpose suite into
   this studio’s, and it belongs in the repository as issues or pull requests.

Worth revisiting this document when:

- A capability is added to or removed from

- The install routes in [INSTALL.md](../INSTALL.md) change, which would date slide 15
- An objection comes up twice in a real session and is not listed above
